An Open, Reproducible Platform for Small-Area Community Indicators: The Carolinas Regional Explorer

A working paper documenting the architecture, methodology, and replication of a civic data platform for the Charlotte region

Kailas Venkitasubramanian, Nick Occhipinti, Fatimah Alnahash

University of North Carolina at Charlotte

Read the paper (PDF) Code & data

Abstract

Most community-indicator dashboards hide the statistical uncertainty of their small-area estimates and do not publish the code that produces them, so their numbers are hard to audit or reproduce. We present the Carolinas Regional Explorer (CRXP), an open platform for census-tract indicators across the 14-county Charlotte region: an open-source web application and a documented, config-driven data pipeline, joined by a versioned data contract. CRXP carries uncertainty through to the user: it propagates American Community Survey margins of error, flags low-reliability estimates, and tests change only between non-overlapping periods. It also documents choices that such platforms usually leave unstated, among them harmonizing 2010 and 2020 tract boundaries by population-weighted interpolation, detecting spatial clusters with FDR-controlled Local Moran's I on rank-normal scores, and attributing vernacular neighborhood names to tracts from OpenStreetMap. Plain-language, cited briefs and open downloads serve non-expert users. We document the system so that other regions can replicate or adapt it with attribution.
